    Models and effort¶

    Each seat runs on the model and effort its work needs. The planner charter holds one table for every repository running WUWEI (charters/planner.md, "Model and effort"). The planner follows it, departs from it with a recorded reason, and is never refused. WUWEI advises; you decide.

    Why: a seat on too weak a model costs fix rounds and escaped defects; a seat on too strong a setting costs tokens for nothing. One written table makes the choice the same each time, and the retro can measure it.

    The inputs¶

    The table reads only signals WUWEI already computes for every item:

    • the item's tier (light, standard, full), after the change failure rate rule raises it a step;
    • risky, owner or contract paths: the risky paths, the owner paths and the contract files the diff touches (a wider set than the trust paths that raise a light item to standard);
    • the item's history: failed fix rounds, gate blockers the builder missed, a park for a design problem, a one-way decision.

    Why each row¶

    Seat Setting Why
    lead Opus 5.5 xhigh ranking and red-teaming the plan is long-horizon reasoning, and a wrong plan wastes every seat after it
    builder Sonnet 5.5 medium at light, Opus 5.5 medium at standard, Opus 5.5 high at full or on risky, owner or contract paths Sonnet 5.5 is Anthropic's choice for everyday coding; Opus 5.5 for complex agentic coding; those paths are intelligence-sensitive
    sentinel-security Opus 5.5 high, xhigh at full or on risky, owner or contract paths a missed trust defect is the costliest escape
    sentinel-arch Opus 5.5 high coupling and contracts need reasoning across the whole design
    sentinel-quality Sonnet 5.5 high, Opus 5.5 high at full or on risky, owner or contract paths a second model family on routine heads; the stronger model where a missed test costs most
    sentinel-goal Sonnet 5.5 high reads the promise against the diff; a different model than the builder at full
    shepherd Sonnet 5.5 medium, Opus 5.5 high for a conflict where both sides changed logic most PR work is routine; a logic conflict is not
    steward Opus 5.5 high the retro weighs evidence across the day
    lookups and searches Haiku 5.5 low Anthropic names Haiku 5.5 for simple, speed-critical work and low effort for simple subagent tasks

    Where two settings in a row apply, the later one holds. The session's own model is yours. The table never names it.

    Escalation and diversity¶

    The ladder runs Haiku 5.5, Sonnet 5.5, Opus 5.5 at medium, high and xhigh, then Fable 5.1. A seat moves up one step after a failed fix round or after a gate finds a blocker the builder missed. Fable 5.1, Anthropic's most capable model, is the step for an item parked as a design problem (two failed fix rounds) and for a one-way decision. Under zero data retention Fable may be unavailable; there the top step is Opus 5.5 at max. A step never lowers effort: Sonnet 5.5 at high steps to Opus 5.5 at high.

    A seat class steps down only on retro evidence: 10 merged items on the lower setting with no escaped defect. The count makes the rule testable.

    At least one gate per head runs on a different model than the builder, so the reviews do not share every blind spot. When the table would put every gate on the builder's model (a light item, or a standard item on risky, owner or contract paths), the quality gate takes the other of Opus 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5, at high.

    After a fix, a delta is re-read by one gate, and the passes from the earlier head carry to it. Those carried passes count toward diversity, so the gate that re-reads keeps its own model. The limit: if the gate on the other model passed the earlier head and the fix changed what it read, the delta has no fresh review from another model.

    Haiku 5.5, the weakest tier, never runs a gate on risky, owner or contract paths. That floor is not a departure the planner can record with a reason: breaking it is an exception: for you.

    Effort¶

    • Set effort on every call. Opus 5.5 defaults to medium, and defaults differ between models and between the API and Claude Code (Sonnet 5.5 is high on the API, medium in Claude Code).
    • Use at least high for intelligence-sensitive work, max when correctness matters more than cost, and low for simple subagent tasks.
    • Before moving a seat to a cheaper model, measure the stronger model at a lower effort. Anthropic reports that tuning effort is often a better lever than switching models.
    • Judge cost per completed item, not per request: a cheap seat that needs a second round costs more.

    How Claude Code applies it¶

    • The planner passes model and effort on each Agent call, with full model IDs (claude-opus-5-5, claude-sonnet-5-5, claude-haiku-5-5, claude-fable-5-1). A family alias such as opus can resolve to the session's own model when it is in that family, and sonnet resolves to an older version on some providers. The per-call effort parameter needs Claude Code v2.1.292 or later.
    • On Amazon Bedrock, Google Cloud's Agent Platform and Microsoft Foundry, model IDs are the provider's own, so claude-sonnet-5-5 may not resolve and the seat can fall back to the session's model without notice. There, set your models rows to the provider's IDs, or pass the aliases with ANTHROPIC_DEFAULT_OPUS_MODEL, ANTHROPIC_DEFAULT_SONNET_MODEL and ANTHROPIC_DEFAULT_HAIKU_MODEL pinned.
    • The brief's first line is Model: <model> <effort> (<row>). A departure adds Model reason: <one line>.
    • Model precedence: the call's model parameter, then the agent file's model frontmatter, then CLAUDE_CODE_SUBAGENT_MODEL, then the session model. Four cases change that order:
    • setting CLAUDE_CODE_SUBAGENT_MODEL_FORCE puts the variable first;
    • before Claude Code v2.1.251, the variable came first anyway;
    • an installed mod that sets a model in its agent.spawn hook replaces the per-call parameter;
    • a value your organization's availableModels blocks is substituted: a family alias takes the newest permitted version of that family, and a full ID runs on the session's model.
    • Effort precedence: CLAUDE_CODE_EFFORT_LEVEL beats every other effort setting, the per-call parameter included. A workspace that sets it turns WUWEI's effort advice off. A managed maxEffortLevel, or an organization effort cap, still limits the level a seat runs at.
    • What a hook can see: the hooks page lists model in the Agent call's tool_input and not effort, though a session transcript of 2026-10-18 shows the call's input holds effort when the planner passes it. The brief's first line records the effort asked for either way. What ran is visible too: the PostToolUse response carries resolvedModel, the model the seat started on, and PreToolUse, PostToolUse, Stop and SubagentStop inputs carry effort.level, the level in effect (also $CLAUDE_EFFORT in Bash).
    • wuwei agents build writes each role's default (the row's first setting, standard for the builder) into its agent file as model: and effort:, so a launch with no model still runs on the table.
    • On each Agent launch the hook compares the role, model and effort with the table at the item's tier (from the brief's Item: line, raised one step when the cached change failure rate says so). On a departure with no Model reason: line it adds a note naming the table's setting; it never refuses or rewrites the call. It cannot see the diff's paths at launch, so a setting the row gives on risky, owner or contract paths, or for a logic conflict, counts as a fit. A Haiku gate gets the note even with a reason line or a models row.
    • Each launch records the model that ran, the model and effort asked for, the reason and the item (unrecorded or unmeasured where the hook cannot read them). A gate's verdict row copies the model and effort.
    • Your own models rows in .wuwei/config.toml override any table row, for example models = {"builder light" = "opus medium"}. A row for a role and tier wins over one for the role alone. A models row that puts Haiku 5.5 on a gate does not break the floor: only your exception: answer does.
    • wuwei doctor names CLAUDE_CODE_EFFORT_LEVEL and CLAUDE_CODE_SUBAGENT_MODEL in a note row when they are set. A note never changes the doctor's exit code. With the first set, the hook leaves effort out of its note; with the second, the model.
    • /wuwei-watch may run on Sonnet 5.5 at medium effort through its own frontmatter. The skill offers that as an option, not a default.

    On Codex¶

    The same table holds on Codex. The mapping below is the planner's reading of the Codex catalog, not OpenAI guidance on seats. Delivery outcomes for this policy remain unmeasured.

    Claude Codex
    Fable 5.1 gpt-6-astra
    Opus 5.5 gpt-6.1-sol
    Sonnet 5.5 gpt-6-luna
    Haiku 5.5 gpt-6-luna low

    The mapping chooses one native model per table family and preserves its effort. Sonnet maps to Luna, which gives routine review seats a different model from an Opus-mapped Sol builder. The planner still chooses a different gate model when the item's selected settings share one model.

    • wuwei init --codex writes model and model_reasoning_effort into each role file. Files use the standard tier because they have no item context. Owner models rows use the existing precedence: <role> standard, then <role>, then the mapped table default. Regenerate after changing rows; wuwei doctor compares against the same configured output.
    • For an item's tier, choose <role> <tier>, then <role>, then the mapped tier setting. Native owner IDs pass through as requests. Availability and provider substitution are unmeasured until Codex supplies resolution metadata. Claude family names in a row use the mapping above.
    • Effort keeps the same names. Sol and Astra support through ultra; Luna stops at max. The installed catalog read on 2026-10-27 defaults Sol to low and Astra and Luna to medium. Role files set effort explicitly. This does not change the owner session's model or effort.
    • Inspect the active spawn_agent schema. This session exposes agent_type, model, reasoning_effort and fork_turns. Other surfaces may omit overrides. A full-history fork inherits settings and does not accept model or effort overrides here. Use per-call settings only when supported, and state the requested setting in the brief's Model: line otherwise. Generated role settings are defaults; neither a request nor a fork policy proves what ran.
    • The hook's note names the tier policy and requested settings without claiming enforcement. asked and effort record requests from the call or brief. model records only the child's supplied resolvedModel, else unmeasured. The launching parent's turn model and effort have separate fields and report labels. They never supply a missing child model.
    • The report labels requested, resolved child, launching parent turn, and fork policy separately. Explicit fork_turns=all means a requested inherit policy; none means fresh. An omitted or unknown value stays unmeasured. Native telemetry supplies no answer, stop or verdict attestation.

    Learning from it¶

    At each close the steward reads outcomes by seat, model and effort. The report's Learning section gives one line for each: launches, fix rounds per item, blocking findings, escapes traced to a passing gate and tokens, and how many launches had no readable model or effort. Where the numbers back it, the retro proposes a change to a row, with those numbers, as advice. See the learning loop.
